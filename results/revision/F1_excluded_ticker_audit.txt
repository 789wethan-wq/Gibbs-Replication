========================================================================================
F1 OVERALL SYNTHESIS
========================================================================================
Direction check against the spec's hypothesis ("if the excluded firms are higher-DeltaS
than the panel average, say so and give the magnitude"): NOT CONFIRMED. Both the
8-quarter FF3-residual proxy (-16.5% relative) and the raw-SD proxy (-15.6% overall,
-17.1% for the very-shortest-lived subset) show excluded firms have LOWER measured
volatility than included firms, not higher. This does not fit the anticipated
narrative and is reported as found rather than reframed to fit it. Two candidate
explanations, neither resolved here: (a) many excluded firms are thinly-traded/
illiquid over their brief life, which can artificially compress measured return
volatility (a stale-pricing artifact, not a genuine low-risk signal) -- distinct
from the entropy story either way; (b) the excluded population may be dominated by
small, quickly-acquired-or-delisted firms whose short life reflects something other
than classic high-disorder distress.

The terminal-quarter return finding points the OTHER direction: excluded-delisted
firms' terminal returns average -0.91% vs included-delisted firms' +3.96% (a 4.87pp
gap), consistent with a worse-outcome population. The two proxies (ex-ante volatility
level vs ex-post terminal outcome) are not measuring the same thing and give a mixed
picture -- report both, do not force a single-direction story.

Combined with E6 (filing-vs-listing survivorship, monotone in DeltaS quintile) and the
0.482%/month terminal-return proxy's own M&A caveat (prior rounds), this IS a third
exclusion margin worth naming, but its direction on DeltaS LEVEL is not simply
confirmatory -- the honest framing is 'three named exclusion margins, mixed evidence
on direction for this one,' not 'three margins all pointing the same way.'
