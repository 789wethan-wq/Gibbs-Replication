DECISION 1: RETITLE away from the Gibbs/thermodynamic framing to the
survivorship-clause framing; compress Sections 2.1-2.4.

Reasoning: after E1 (dispersion-normalized placebos), no empirical content
motivated by the Gibbs equation survives:
  - H3 (regime-conditional loadings) already unsupported (prior rounds)
  - the enthalpy/entropy unit-consistency ratio already untestable (prior rounds)
  - the structural ΔG = ΔH - TΔS interpretation already withdrawn in V31
  - the residual "directional conditional-pricing observation" (the last
    surviving piece) shown by E1 to be a generic property shared by (and in
    several cases stronger in) unrelated placebo characteristics -- not
    specific to entropy/disorder.
The ΔH/ΔS decomposition remains useful as a pair of empirical variables; the
survivorship-correction finding (the paper's actual strongest, most novel
contribution) does not need thermodynamic framing to motivate or state. A
title promising a theoretical framework that the paper's own internal tests
have withdrawn every empirical claim for is a framing risk a referee will
flag. Consistent with the chat instance's own restored recommendation
(reversing its round-3 decision to keep the framing, on the grounds that
that judgment assumed one surviving pattern, which no longer holds).

DECISION 2: CUT R19's synthetic-delisting stress-test/sufficiency framing.
KEEP the E7 calibrated finding (at the measured 0.482%/month delisting rate,
the premium attenuates from +13.36%/yr to +8.68%/yr, t=+3.93 to +2.58 --
realistic delisting explains roughly a third of the unconditional premium).

Reasoning: the original V38 spec pre-committed a decision rule for this
exact scenario -- "if it does not [lower the required rate], that is also
worth knowing, and R19 should probably be cut rather than defended a fourth
time." E7 found the monotone-hazard design requires a HIGHER top-quartile
hazard (5.25%/mo) than R19's uniform-quartile design implied (4.00%/mo,
itself already ~8x the measured rate, not the ~4x the manuscript's text
claims due to an arithmetic error caught in E7). A sufficiency bound built
on an assumed hazard rate is inherently more contestable than a point
estimate at the actually-measured rate, and this is the fourth round of
exactly that contestation. The E7 calibrated number needs no assumption-
stacking and stands on its own.
