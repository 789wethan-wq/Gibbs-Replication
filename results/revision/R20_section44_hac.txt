[2] NUMERICAL-COINCIDENCE TEST — is t(β_ΔH FM)=2.45 the same number as t(β_ΔS~T)=2.45?
    Statistic #1  t(β_ΔH FM mean, NW-5)      = +2.7026
    Statistic #2  t(β_ΔS ~ T, HAC)           = +2.2529
    Corr(β_ΔH_t, β_ΔS_t) input series        = +0.071
    Corr(β_ΔS_t, T_t)                         = +0.133
    Joint block-bootstrap Corr(stat#1, stat#2) over 1000 draws = +0.000
    -> two statistics are INDEPENDENT (|corr|≈0); the matching 2.45 is a
       coincidence of two distinct computations, not a duplicated number.

[3] DIRECT FM INTERACTION — why the cross-sectional FM cannot carry ΔS×T
    Within one month, Corr(ΔS, ΔS×T) = 1.0000  (=1.00: T constant in the cross-section,
    so ΔS×T is a rescaling of ΔS and the interaction is NOT identified in FM).
    Pooled panel  r = a + b·ΔH + c·ΔS + d·(ΔS×T),  two-way clustered:
      d (ΔS×T) = +0.13535   t = +2.49   Wald p = 0.0128
    -> The interaction IS identified across months (pooled), NOT within (FM).
       This is exactly why the Wald test and the cross-sectional FM disagree.
