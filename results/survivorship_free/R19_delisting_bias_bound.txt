INTERPRETATION
  Survivor-only (δ=0) reproduces the paper headline:
    ΔG L/S = -14.3%/yr (t=-3.60)   [paper: -13.4%/yr, t=-3.70]
    disorder (ΔS) L/S = +13.4%/yr (t=+3.93)

  Under synthetic delisting of the most-distressed survivors, the
  disorder premium does NOT attenuate gently — it collapses and REVERSES:
    δ= 0.0%/mo  →  disorder L/S =  +13.4%/yr (t=+3.93)
    δ= 0.5%/mo  →  disorder L/S =   +0.3%/yr (t=+0.10)
    δ= 1.0%/mo  →  disorder L/S =   -9.0%/yr (t=-3.05)
    δ= 2.0%/mo  →  disorder L/S =  -24.3%/yr (t=-5.64)
    δ= 5.0%/mo  →  disorder L/S =  -76.1%/yr (t=-9.47)

  → The +13.4%/yr premium is fully eliminated at a delisting rate of
    only ~1.0%/mo among high-disorder firms — well within
    empirically observed rates for distressed / high-iVol deciles
    (~0.4-1%/mo). This bounds the SURVIVORSHIP COMPONENT of the
    unconditional disorder premium: it is the decisive marginal driver
    that pushes the premium through zero, but NOT the whole story.

  This agrees with R18 (actual delisted firms), where the ΔS premium
  vanishes outright (FM t = +0.02). The controlled one-variable-at-a-time
  decomposition (robustness/DIAG_survivorship.py -> DIAG_Q1Q3.md) splits
  that collapse: ~60% is UNIVERSE BREADTH (the premium is a large-cap
  S&P500 result that does not generalize to the full US-common universe),
  ~30% is survivorship among the mostly small-cap delisted tail, ~8% is
  frequency. So the honest framing is 'survivorship + non-generalization',
  not a pure survivorship artifact. Because the SF1 quarterly panel applies
  no true Shumway delisting return, the ~30% survivorship share is a LOWER
  BOUND and this synthetic stress test is the complementary upper bound.

  CAVEAT: synthetic stress on survivors bounds the bias direction and
  magnitude; it is not a point estimate. The empirical point estimate
  (real delisted firms) is in R18_sf1_quarterly_results.txt.
